1 History
Ercolano · ~20 min from the airport
Herculaneum — Pompeii's quieter twin

Buried by the same Vesuvius eruption in 79 AD but far less visited — which in late June is a significant advantage. Herculaneum was a wealthy seaside resort town; the sudden pyroclastic surge (rather than slow ash fall) preserved wooden furniture, food, textiles and mosaics in extraordinary condition. The site is compact and can be covered thoroughly in 1.5–2 hours without feeling rushed. Save Pompeii for Day 2 when you have proper time.

1.5–2 hours visit
Opens 9:00 AM (last entry 17:30)
Corso Resina, Ercolano — parking nearby
☀️ Late June heat warning: Herculaneum is entirely open-air with minimal shade. Aim for the 9:00 opening. Bring a sun hat, SPF 50+, and at least 1.5 L of water per person. The heat peaks between 12:00–15:00 — you want to be driving or indoors by then.
🏛️ Don't miss: The Neptune & Amphitrite mosaic in the Casa del Mosaico di Nettuno; the carbonised wooden cradle and furniture in Casa del Salone Nero; and the ancient boat sheds on the old shoreline, where skeletal remains of fleeing residents are still visible.
2 Scenic
SS145 coastal road · 10 min south of Herculaneum
Driving Past Vesuvius — a Photo Stop

Heading south on the SS145 between Torre del Greco and Castellammare di Stabia, you drive with Vesuvius on the left and the Bay of Naples on the right — one of the most visually dramatic stretches of road in Italy. Find a piazzola di sosta (a roadside layby) for a 10-minute photo stop. The sight of an active volcano hanging above a modern city never loses its impact.

10–15 min
Free — roadside pull-off
Best views between Torre del Greco and Torre Annunziata
3 Lunch + Shop
Castellammare di Stabia · ~10 min further south
Lunch on the Seafront + Supermarket

Castellammare di Stabia is a working Neapolitan coastal town — not a tourist destination. That means local prices, local food, and real atmosphere. Park near the waterfront promenade (Viale Europa) and find a trattoria for a proper sit-down lunch. Afterward, the Conad supermarket on the main road is your best opportunity to stock up for the week before entering the more expensive peninsula towns.

~1 hour for lunch
+20 min supermarket
Viale Europa waterfront promenade
🍽️ What to look for: Any trattoria with Italians eating inside and a handwritten menù del giorno on a blackboard. That's always the best sign. Budget €12–18 per person for a full primo, secondo and a carafe of house wine.

4 Arrival
Via Capo 60, Sorrento · ~40 min from Castellammare
Via Capo — Clifftop Sorrento

Via Capo is the dramatic coastal road that runs west from Sorrento's historic centre along the clifftops toward Capo di Sorrento. Hotels here sit high above the sea with panoramic views across the Bay of Naples — this is where you catch those iconic shots of Vesuvius at sunset. The road is narrow but driveable; follow your navigation carefully into the hotel entrance. Once parked, leave the car and walk everywhere in Sorrento itself.

Aim to arrive by 16:00–17:00
Narrow hotel access road — go slowly
~25 min walk to Piazza Tasso on foot

🅿️ Critical: Parking at Via Capo

Confirm with your hotel whether they have on-site parking before arrival — most Via Capo hotels have limited spaces, sometimes charged separately (~€15–20/night). Book your parking spot in advance if possible. Once at the hotel, do not move the car to explore Sorrento — walk down into town instead. Sorrento's centre has very limited non-resident parking and aggressive ZTL enforcement on several approach roads.

⚠️ ZTL into Sorrento centre: Do NOT attempt to drive to Piazza Tasso or Corso Italia. The ZTL zone covers most of the historic centre and is camera-enforced at all entry points. Walk down from Via Capo (~25 min) or take the local bus (SITA line from the Via Capo stop).
🌅 First evening: After settling in, walk down into Sorrento for dinner. Piazza Tasso is the lively main square — full of restaurants and bars. For something calmer and more local, head to the area around Via Luigi de Maio and the fishermen's port below. In late June the sun sets around 20:30 — the Via Capo clifftop view at golden hour over Vesuvius is unforgettable.

Driving Notes — Day 1
🚦 ZTL Zones

Red circle signs reading "ZTL" = camera-enforced no-entry for non-residents. Fines arrive by post 4–6 weeks later. Sorrento's centre has ZTL on Corso Italia, Via San Cesareo, and all approaches to Piazza Tasso. Always walk into the centre from Via Capo.

🅿️ Rental car security

Rental cars are targeted for break-ins throughout Campania. Never leave bags, chargers, passports or any visible items in the car — even in hotel car parks. Use the hotel safe for valuables overnight.

🛣️ Via Capo road width

Via Capo itself is two-lane but the side roads and hotel entrances branching off it can be very tight. Approach slowly. If meeting oncoming traffic in a narrow section, one car must reverse to a wider spot — be patient and let the other car through.

🌡️ Late June traffic

Multiply every Waze estimate by 1.5× on the coastal SS145 and any roads into Sorrento in late June. The A3 motorway is reliable. Budget extra time on the peninsula approach — weekend afternoons are especially congested.

⛽ Fuel strategy

Fill up at Castellammare di Stabia during the supermarket stop. Petrol stations in Sorrento and on the peninsula charge a premium and can be awkward to access in a rental car. Keep the tank at least half full at all times.

🇮🇹 Southern driving culture

Aggressive overtaking, close following, and frequent horn use are normal in Campania — especially in summer. Don't retaliate and don't let it rattle you. Locals are usually more irritable in peak tourist season. Deep breath, keep left when slower.
